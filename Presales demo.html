Case Study — Keka HRTech

Increasing Top-of-Funnel with High-Intent Product Demo Experiences

How service design thinking transformed lead quality and sales efficiency at a fast-growing SaaS platform.

Service Design & Growth Strategy
Keka (HRTech SaaS)
2025
The Context

01 — The Problem

Marketing Acquisition vs. Meaningful Discovery

At Keka, a fast-growing HRTech SaaS platform, website traffic was healthy but a significant portion of leads entering the funnel lacked real product intent. Traditional top-of-funnel experiences created friction while attracting curiosity-driven visitors rather than buyers.

How might we allow potential customers to experience the product earlier in the journey while improving lead quality?

Sales teams were spending time qualifying leads with little understanding of the product. This created a gap between marketing acquisition and meaningful product discovery.

  • 📊
    High Volume, Low Intent Leads Many leads filled demo forms without understanding the product or having genuine purchase intent.
  • ⏱
    Sales Bottleneck Teams spent significant time explaining basic workflows before real qualification could begin.
  • 🔒
    Limited Product Exposure The website described features but didn't allow users to experience the product value firsthand.
  • 🚪
    Delayed Product Discovery Users only saw the product after booking a sales demo, evaluating the promise rather than the product itself.
The Approach

02 — Strategic Approach

Service Design Meets Growth Strategy

I approached the challenge through the lens of service design and growth strategy, focusing on understanding the complete customer journey and identifying critical friction points.

  • → Mapping the product discovery journey from awareness to demo request
  • → Identifying specific points of friction and drop-off in the user flow
  • → Designing a self-serve product experience layer between marketing and sales
  • → Ensuring the solution improved both lead quantity and quality simultaneously
01
Service Blueprint Mapping
Created a comprehensive service blueprint of the lead acquisition flow, mapping frontstage customer experiences and backstage operations.
02
Critical Insight Discovery
Customers were being asked to commit to a sales conversation before they had a chance to meaningfully explore the product.
03
Solution Design
Introduced a low-friction product discovery layer that allowed customers to experience product value before committing.

Reflections

05 — Key Takeaways

What This Taught Me

Product experience beats product description
Allowing users to interact with the product earlier in the journey creates more qualified leads than any amount of marketing copy or feature lists.
Self-qualification improves both sides
When users can assess fit themselves, sales teams get better leads and users get more valuable conversations. It's a win-win through better information flow.
Service design reveals hidden friction
Mapping the complete journey from awareness to demo revealed that we were asking for commitment before providing value — a fundamental mismatch.
Product-led growth starts with access
The shift from information-led to product-led discovery wasn't about removing sales — it was about empowering users to make informed decisions earlier.
