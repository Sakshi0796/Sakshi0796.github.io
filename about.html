The Problem Space

01 — Problem

Every March, the Same Failure Surfaced

At KekaHR, a recurring seasonal pattern revealed a deep gap in the product experience. Users weren't failing to use the product — they were failing to know what to do before reaching out for help. The ticket wasn't the problem. The moment before the ticket was.

"The real problem wasn't that users needed help. It was that the product wasn't helping them before they even knew they needed it."
— Core Insight, Journey Thinking Framework

Pain Points Identified

  • 🔁
    Seasonal Repetition The same support queries spiked every March, signalling a systemic, not incidental, gap in the user journey.
  • 🧩
    Invisible Friction Points Users encountered blockers that weren't obvious in the UI — no guidance, no contextual help, no next step.
  • ⏱
    Support Team Overload Repetitive, preventable tickets consumed agent bandwidth, slowing down response times for genuinely complex issues.
  • 📉
    User Trust Erosion Every avoidable ticket was a micro-moment of product failure, quietly chipping away at user confidence in KekaHR.
The Approach

02 — Process

Designing Backwards from the Ticket

Instead of optimising the support flow itself, the approach was to map the user journey before ticket creation — and redesign the moments that were generating frustration.

01
Pattern Recognition
Analysed support ticket data to identify recurring themes, seasonal clusters, and the user journeys that consistently ended in a ticket submission.
02
Journey Mapping
Reconstructed the user's path 5–10 steps before the ticket moment — identifying exact UI touchpoints where confusion or friction built up unnoticed.
03
Intervention Design
Designed in-context nudges, proactive help content, and UI micro-improvements targeting each identified failure point in the pre-ticket journey.
04
Hypothesis Testing
Validated each intervention with targeted rollouts, measuring ticket volume impact at each friction point before scaling solutions.
05
Cross-Team Alignment
Worked across product, support, and customer success to ensure that insights fed back into both the roadmap and support playbooks simultaneously.
06
Scaling the Framework
Codified the "Journey Thinking" approach as a repeatable framework for all future support-adjacent product decisions at KekaHR.

Reflections

05 — Learnings

What This Taught Me

Support data is a product signal, not just an ops problem
Tickets are not noise to be managed. They're a map of where the product fails users silently. Reading them with a product eye unlocks insights that roadmap reviews miss entirely.
The most valuable intervention is the one users never notice
If the contextual help works, users don't think "great, the product helped me." They just move forward. Success is invisible friction removal — and that's the hardest thing to measure and celebrate.
Seasonality is a design constraint, not an anomaly
Recurring patterns signal that the product hasn't designed for the full user calendar. Building for March means building for every predictable peak in a customer's year.
Frameworks outlive features
The ticket deflection numbers mattered. But the lasting value was the "Journey Thinking" lens — a way of asking "what happens before the problem?" that the team now uses by default.
